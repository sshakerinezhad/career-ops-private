## C) Level and Strategy

### JD level vs natural level

"Member of Technical Staff" is deliberately flat. There is no ladder, no YoE floor, no degree requirement and no publication requirement. At a five-person org, MTS means "one of the people who does the work", which is the honest description of what he is. There is no level mismatch to manage here, which is itself worth noting: most of the reqs in this pipeline force a Senior/Staff conversation and this one does not.

### Screen risk, plainly

The application form (read from Ashby, six fields) is this:

- Name, Email: required
- "Which role do you think you'd be a good fit for?": required, multi-select across all three MTS reqs
- **Resume: OPTIONAL**
- **Your LinkedIn or personal website: REQUIRED**
- "How did you hear about this opportunity?": required
- "Briefly, why do you think you'd be a good fit for this role?": optional long text

That inverts the normal screen and it changes the risk profile completely. The three rejections so far were cold résumé screens at frontier labs; the one advance (Mercor) came through a take-home. This is neither, but it is much closer to the second: the résumé is optional and the required artifact is a link to a website. **The screen is not "does his CV parse", it is "does the linked page make an agent-eval engineer legible within about ten seconds to a busy person at a five-person org."**

Concrete risks, in order:

1. **The required link is the whole screen and it is currently off-message.** sshakerinezhad.github.io is a Win95-style React site. It is genuinely good and it hits the UX nice-to-have, but a reader looking for agent and eval work has to dig. LinkedIn is the safer default only if the profile leads with the BMO eval work and the LessWrong post. Fix the link target before submitting; this is the single highest-leverage 30 minutes in the whole application.
2. **The optional free-text is functionally mandatory.** With a five-person team and an open call amplified on X, the "briefly, why" box is the only place differentiated signal can land. Leaving it blank is the same as not applying.
3. **Volume risk.** The X post (2026-08-28) plus Shoshannah Tekofsky's amplification means this got broad reach in the AI-safety-adjacent crowd, which is exactly the population that self-selects into this posting. Expect a large, high-quality applicant pool with no structured rubric filtering it. Timing matters; the req has been open since 2026-08-13.
4. **Employment structure is unstated.** A US 501(c)(3) hiring a Toronto-based person remote is most likely a contractor arrangement. That is not a blocker and it is not hidden, it is just unaddressed. It affects benefits, payroll taxes and how the $150-350k number should be read.
5. **Capacity.** Full-time BMO plus an M.Eng running to April 2027. Have the answer ready before the first call, not during it.

### Selling seniority without lying

The lever is not title, it is autonomy. The posting says "you'll likely work fairly independently and with little code review/oversight." The honest, verifiable claim is that he has already been doing precisely that: co-founded a three-person collective with no funding and no supervision and shipped an 8th-place challenge result, a technical report, a LessWrong analysis and a merged open-source contribution, in parallel with a full-time job. That is a seniority argument that needs no title inflation.

Second lever: he has found real failures in deployed systems rather than described the possibility of finding them. The BMO framing per `modes/_profile.md`: built the evaluation harnesses and test setups that caught an agentic tool serving $200B+ AUM subtly downplaying investment risk, behaviour that was invisible until scaled to hundreds of test cases. Do not lead with the greeter robot. Do not describe Merlyn as anything beyond a three-person self-organized research collective.

### If they downlevel

There is no level to be downleveled to. The equivalent risk is landing at the bottom of a range that spans 2.3x. Handle it as a band-position conversation:

> "Based on market data for research engineering roles at this level, I'm targeting [range from profile.yml]. I'm flexible on structure; what matters is total package and the research agenda."

And if the geography discount comes up:

> "My output is location-independent: published research, open-source contributions, and production systems speak for themselves. I'm benchmarking against US-market rates for this role."

Anchor above the midpoint. The band top ($350k) exceeds his robotics-startup target, so there is genuine room, and a nonprofit that publishes its range openly is unlikely to punish a well-argued ask.

- **Likely guaranteed base:** the number is presented flatly as "the salary range", with no OTE, bonus, commission, variable, or "up to" language, and a nonprofit has no equity to fold in. Read it as base cash. That is a cleaner signal than most startup ranges in this pipeline.

Grant-funded comp is reliable while the grant is; that is a different risk shape from a startup's, not a worse one, but it is not a public-company band either.

**Demand trend:** the "measure what frontier agents actually do over long horizons" niche is expanding fast and hiring is concentrated in a handful of small orgs (METR, Apollo, Redwood, Palisade, AI Digest, plus lab-internal eval teams). Demand for people who can both build agent scaffolding and analyze the resulting behavioural data is genuinely higher than supply, and this profile sits in the overlap. Levels.fyi and Blind have no usable data for Sage or AI Digest; Glassdoor returns a stub with no disclosed figures. Treat the JD's own number as the best available source.

5. What is Sage's current funded runway, and how is this role's funding sourced? Is it covered by committed grants or by the in-progress 2026-2027 raise?
6. Are salaries reviewed on a cycle, and is there any published internal band structure?

**Comp score: 4/5.** All-cash, no equity dilution risk, a midpoint of $250k that clears the $180k floor comfortably and would be paid to a Toronto resident. Per the location policy, remote-from-Toronto at US comp is the dream scenario and scores generously. Held below 5 because realistic band placement is lower-middle rather than midpoint, because there is no equity upside at all, and because grant funding plus an active raise puts a real question mark over the out-years.
