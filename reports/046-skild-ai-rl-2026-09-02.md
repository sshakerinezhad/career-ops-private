### Gaps and mitigation

**1. RL algorithm breadth (model-free, model-based, multi-task, hierarchical, multi-agent).**
- Hard blocker? No. It sits under Preferred, and the JD's own framing ("varying levels of experience... from new graduates to domain experts... demonstrated abilities") explicitly invites non-linear backgrounds.
- Adjacent experience? Substantial but sideways. The RLinf contribution is RL infrastructure for VLA policies; the VLM judge work is reward engineering. Both are things RL teams need and few candidates have. Neither is "I implemented SAC, PPO, and a model-based variant and can tell you when each breaks."
- Portfolio proof point? RLinf on GitHub is the strongest single artifact here because it is externally verifiable and it is RL-specific.
- Mitigation: lead with the infrastructure and reward angle rather than pretending to algorithm breadth. "I have not shipped a hierarchical RL agent. What I have shipped is the integration that lets a flow-matching VLA train under RL on BEHAVIOR-1K, and reward models that do not fall over when the policy games them." Then close the gap before the loop: the CoderPad round at Skild reportedly includes writing torch components from scratch, so a week of implementing PPO and a model-based baseline against a MuJoCo task is both interview prep and a legitimate claim upgrade.

**2. "Extensive industry experience with RL and robotic systems."**
- Hard blocker? No, but semi-hard per Calibration Rule 3, and it is the sentence most likely to end this application at the screen.
- Adjacent experience? The research output is genuinely frontier-adjacent and externally verifiable, which is the argument that survives contact with a hiring manager. It is a weaker argument against a recruiter filtering mechanically for years-in-industry.
- Mitigation: a warm path, not better wording. See Block C and the next action.

**3. RL versus imitation learning weighting in the headline results.**
- Hard blocker? No.
- The BEHAVIOR-1K result, the LIBERO-PRO study and the AlohaMini transfer are imitation-learning and finetuning results. Presenting them as RL results would be exactly the tool-of-trade conflation the source-of-truth rules forbid.
- Mitigation: state the boundary out loud. "Most of my published robot-learning work is imitation and post-training. The RL side of my work is infrastructure and reward design." Volunteering the boundary buys credibility for everything else on the page.
