- Mitigation: route around, not through. The campus siblings carry no YoE bullet at all (Block C). If applying to this req anyway, answer YoE dropdowns 2-4 per the standing decision and do not editorialize.

**2. The publication record bullet.**
- Blocker or nice-to-have: **treat as binding on this req**, per rule 4. It is under a required-flavored header, it names five specific venues, and there is no open-source escape clause.
- Adjacent: a complete paper with a falsification structure, a technical report tied to a public leaderboard result, a merged open-source contribution, and a LessWrong analysis.
- Mitigation, two moves, neither instant. First, prefer the sibling reqs whose equivalent bullet accepts open-source contributions (8052343) or omits publications entirely (8052313). Second, putting "Recalibrating VLA Baselines" on arXiv converts "unpublished" into "public research with a stable identifier". That is a user decision and it is the highest-leverage single step across this whole class of req, exactly as flagged in report 043. Until then: never say published, accepted, under review, or name a venue.

**3. "Taking RL methods from research into production".**
- Blocker or nice-to-have: nice-to-have as written, but it is the phrase that separates a strong candidate from a very strong one here, because the entire role is described as owning that handoff.
- Adjacent: the BMO RL environments are being built inside a bank for a division with real money attached, which is closer to production than an academic setting. The RLinf contribution is production-grade in the sense that other people now depend on it.
- Mitigation: be precise about stage. "Building RL environments to train agents for a wealth management division" is true. "Shipped an RL policy" is not. Profile rule 6 bans verb inflation and this is exactly where it would be tempting.

**4. Zero finance domain.**
- Blocker or nice-to-have: **genuinely nice-to-have here**, and this is the one gap that is safe to be relaxed about. Jump's own boilerplate says it recruits from Mathematics, Physics and Computer Science and teaches markets in-house. Engineering Physics plus a numerical-methods background is the profile they hire and train.
- Mitigation: do not fake market intuition in an interview. The honest and stronger line is that the RL problem shape is familiar and the domain model is the thing to learn.
