2. **No Inspect experience (soft, explicitly waived by the JD).** *Blocker?* No, the JD says AI domain expertise including "familiarity with evaluation frameworks like Inspect" is "a strong plus but not required." *Adjacent?* He has built eval frameworks from scratch, which is a harder version of learning one. *Portfolio proof?* None. *Mitigation:* Inspect is open source. Spending a weekend porting one small benchmark into Inspect and saying so in the application converts the single named gap into a demonstrated plus, at very low cost. 3. **Benchmark domain is robotics and enterprise, not public LLM capability benchmarks (soft).** *Blocker?* No. *Adjacent?* Strongly. The transferable core is benchmark methodology: knowing that a headline number can be an artifact of the setup rather than the model, and being willing to rebuild the baseline to prove it. That is a rarer skill than knowing a particular eval harness, and it is precisely the failure mode a public benchmarking hub exists to avoid. *Proof point?* article-digest.md §3, which is the single best artifact for this application. *Mitigation:* lead with it. Frame the LIBERO-PRO work as benchmark integrity, not as robotics.
4. **No evidence of provider-integration and harness operations (soft, and the most job-shaped gap).** "Integrate with AI providers" and "quickly and painlessly evaluate new model releases" implies API integration across vendors, rate-limit and cost management, caching, retries, and reproducible reruns. 5. **Paper status discipline (not a gap, a guardrail).** "Recalibrating VLA Baselines" was submitted to CoRL 2026 and rejected, is unpublished, and is not on arXiv. Never write "published," "accepted," "under review," or name a venue. "We wrote a paper" and "proposed an alternative baseline" are the approved framings. This matters more than usual here because the audience is a research institute that will check.
