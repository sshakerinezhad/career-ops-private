### Gaps and mitigation

**1. No visual generative modeling (diffusion / text-to-image).**
Hard blocker? No, but it is the largest real gap. The JD lists diffusion **or** flow models, and flow matching is on the CV with a shipped implementation, so the requirement is literally satisfied. A reviewer skimming for "trained a diffusion model on images" will not find one.
Adjacent experience: flow matching for continuous action generation shares the objective and much of the training machinery with rectified-flow image models. BardSong includes an image-generation stage, but that is API orchestration, not model training. Do not overstate it.
Mitigation: say plainly that the flow-matching work is in the policy setting, then explain the transfer in mechanism terms (the velocity-field objective, sampler schedules, the conditioning path). Naming the boundary is more credible than blurring it, and the JD's "comfort with problems where the answer isn't known in advance" line gives cover for a stated learning curve.

**2. No human-preference-pair or RLHF/DPO experience.**
Hard blocker? No. It is listed under nice-to-have ("preference data or human-feedback systems") and under a broad method menu in the requirements, not as a standalone must.
Adjacent experience: VLM judges that turn rollouts into dense, hard-to-game reward signal. That is reward-model design under the constraint that matters most here, which is preventing the model from satisfying the judge without doing the task.
Portfolio proof point: the BMO bias discovery is the same skill pointed at a deployed system. A reward model trained on designer preferences will absorb whatever systematic slant is in the label distribution, and he has already found exactly that class of failure once at scale.
Mitigation: lead with reward-signal design as the transferable skill. Never write RLHF or RLAIF on the CV or in a form answer. Per `modes/_profile.md` rule 11c those terms are unbacked and their previous appearance is the canonical violation.

**3. No large-scale training-infrastructure credential.**
Hard blocker? No, it is nice-to-have and the JD frames the team as still building this out ("Contribute to reliable training and experimentation infrastructure").
Mitigation: the RLinf contribution is the honest proof of working inside someone else's training stack and leaving it better. That maps directly to the "makes it easier to run, compare, and reproduce experiments" bullet.

**4. Domain distance and the anti-publication stance.**
Not a skills gap, an interest gap. The JD says outright: "If your primary goal is research that ends with publication, this may not be the right environment." Publication cadence is part of his stated identity (technical report, LessWrong, a CoRL submission). This deserves an honest internal answer before applying, not a rehearsed one.
