### Gap analysis and mitigation

**1. PhD plus 8+ years. Hard blocker.**
The req is explicit and the "(or equivalent experience)" escape hatch is attached to the PhD, not to the 8-year number. Equivalent experience to a PhD plus 8 years is a decade of output. There is no honest framing that closes this. Mitigation is not to argue the gap away, it is to route around it: NVIDIA runs many robotics simulation reqs at lower bands (the scan history already carries "Robotics Software Engineer - Robot Simulation and Benchmarking", JR2020637, same team area, same site). Those are the reqs to pursue.

**2. No Isaac Lab / PhysX / Warp / CUDA hands-on. Adjacent, closable.**
OmniGibson runs on Isaac Sim and PhysX, so the candidate has worked on top of NVIDIA's stack without touching its APIs directly. This gap is cheap to close and closing it doubles as the warm path: `github.com/isaac-sim/IsaacLab-Arena` is public, and Arena's own published roadmap says the metrics library is expanding from rule-based methods to neural and agent-based evaluation. A VLM-judge metric contributed there is simultaneously portfolio proof, an Isaac Lab credential, and an introduction to the exact team that owns this req.

**3. No top-venue publication. Soft, and a stated differentiator not a requirement.**
The CoRL 2026 rejection is recent. The work itself is real and defensible; the venue is not there. Never state or imply publication. The honest line is "we wrote a paper proposing an alternative baseline" plus the technical report and the RLinf merge, which are externally verifiable in a way a rejected submission is not.

**4. No platform productization track record. Hard for this specific req, soft for the team.**
The req's core value proposition is "translate research into product-ready Arena capabilities". The candidate's output is research plus internal infrastructure, not a shipped developer platform. This is genuinely a seniority gap rather than a framing gap, and it is the reason to target a build-side req rather than this Research/Product bridge role.
