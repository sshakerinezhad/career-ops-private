## C) Level and Positioning Strategy

**Screen risk, stated plainly.** High. This is a résumé-screen-first process at a lab whose founder is one of the most cited academics in autonomous driving, in the same quarter it announced roughly US$1B in funding, with the req actively sourced from a CVPR recruiting pool. A cold application from a candidate with zero top-tier conference papers, against a pool that will contain CVPR/CoRL/NeurIPS authors with AV-specific work, is very likely filtered before a human reads the BEHAVIOR-1K report. Calibration Rule 3 logic applies by analogy: stated qualifications on a research title are semi-hard because screens apply them mechanically. Calibration Rule 4 applies directly: there is no honest team-norms or title-inflation argument that makes "publications in top-tier conferences" waivable here, because the same requirement appears twice, once as a qualification and once as a duty, and the sourcing tag corroborates it.

**The attainability read (Calibration Rule 7).** The post-screen process is unusually light for a research title: reported public accounts describe a 30-minute recruiter screen, then a 30-minute hiring-manager conversation with a short non-LeetCode coding exercise. That matters. It means almost all of the difficulty is concentrated in getting past the résumé filter, and almost none of it is in surviving the loop. Expected value is therefore dominated by whether a human sees the work. Warm intro is not a nice-to-have here; it is the entire strategy. Waabi is a University of Toronto spinout with a Toronto office, Shayan is a UofT MIE M.Eng student, and his RLinf contribution is publicly attributable. That is a genuine warm path, not a hypothetical one.

**How to sell seniority without lying.** Lead with findings, not titles. "Found that masking 60% of proprioception improved task success by up to 48%" and "showed the published pi-0.5 checkpoint's LIBERO-PRO collapse is recipe-induced rather than architectural, and doubled position-swap success from 21% to 42% with a conservative finetuning recipe" are both research-scientist-shaped claims stated at full strength without a single inflated noun. "We wrote a paper proposing an alternative baseline" is the honest and still-strong framing.

**If they downlevel.** Take it, and say so early rather than being moved. The correct script here is to preempt: "I'm targeting the Research Engineer track at Waabi rather than Research Scientist, my publication record is a technical report and open-source contributions rather than conference papers, and I'd rather be levelled honestly."
