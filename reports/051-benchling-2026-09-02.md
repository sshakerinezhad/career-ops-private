### Gaps and mitigation

**1. Biology-and-AI domain experience. Hard blocker at the screen, and honest to call it that.**

This is not a generic "2+ years of experience" line that a recruiter reads loosely. It names a specific intersection and a specific activity: evaluating and improving scientific models or LLMs *for biological applications*. There is no adjacent experience that converts. Epineuron is the closest thing on the CV and it is a hardware co-op in a medical-device company, so calling it "biology and AI" would be exactly the tool-of-trade conflation the source-of-truth rules forbid. Under Calibration Rule 4, waving this away would need a specific reason it is waivable, and there is not one: the whole point of the team is that the tasks come from real wet-lab work, and someone who cannot read a protocol cannot judge whether a task is well-posed.

The only credible mitigation is to change what is being evaluated. Instead of arguing the qualification is satisfied, show the part of the job that biology does not touch: benchmark calibration, failure-mode experimental design, and judge construction that resists gaming. That case is made by an artifact, not by a résumé line. See Block C.

**2. Wet-lab and molecular-biology literacy. Soft blocker, downstream of gap 1.**

Even past a screen, "turn complex scientific work into rigorous tasks" requires enough domain literacy to know when a task is trivially solvable or ambiguously specified. He would be dependent on the scientists for that judgment for months. This is survivable in a role framed around infrastructure and methodology, and fatal in one framed around task authorship. Worth asking directly which the role actually is.

**3. Data-engineering vocabulary. Soft, mostly a keyword problem.**

"Build scalable data infrastructure ... large volumes of scientific data" is a data-engineering bullet, and the CV has pipelines without the stack names. If real warehouse, orchestration or large-scale ETL work exists from BMO that never made the CV, that is a `cv.md` conversation to have before applying. Per the standing rule, nothing gets added speculatively.

**4. Frontier-lab collaboration. Nice-to-have.**

The JD frames this as partnership work, and the honest answer is open-source and public writeups rather than lab partnerships. The RLinf merge is the strongest single piece of evidence that he can work inside someone else's research codebase and have it accepted.

**5. Paper status. Not a gap, a discipline item.**

"Recalibrating VLA Baselines" is unpublished: submitted to CoRL 2026 and rejected. Never "published", "accepted", "under review", or a named venue anywhere in the application. The honest framing is "we wrote a paper proposing an alternative baseline", and the result stands on its own without a venue.

## C) Level and Strategy

**JD level versus natural level.** Well matched on paper. "Research Engineer" with no level suffix, no degree gate and no publication gate is the right title band for someone one year into a full-time research engineering role with independent output behind it. Calibration Rule 1 does not fire here, which is worth naming because it has fired on the Research Scientist reqs and is the usual reason a strong evals profile dies at the door. Nothing in this posting forces a downlevel conversation on seniority.

**Screen risk, stated plainly (Rules 3 and 4).**

Screen risk on this req is **high**, and it comes from one bullet rather than a cluster:

1. **The bio-x-AI qualification is the first line of the Qualifications list and it is mechanically checkable.** A recruiter or a résumé filter scanning for biology, bioinformatics, computational biology, wet lab, protein, assay or similar terms finds none of them in `cv.md`. Under Rule 3 this class of requirement gets filtered on literally, and the standing correction is never to hand-wave it. There is no title inflation, no team norm and no referral path visible from outside that makes it waivable, so it should be treated as binding.
2. **Volume.** Benchling has 811 employees and 48 live reqs, and this is a visible, well-branded team that just published a public benchmark. That publication is itself an inbound magnet: everyone who reads the blog post and works on evals now knows the team exists.
3. **What cuts the other way:** the remaining five qualifications are soft and he clears all of them convincingly, and the interview loop includes an AI-focused exercise. Benchling is not a résumé-prestige shop. The bar in play is a domain bar, and a domain bar is exactly the kind an artifact can route around because the artifact demonstrates the transferable half in their own subject matter.

**Attainability read (Rule 7).**

The evidence base so far: the one advance came through a take-home (Mercor), and the three rejections were all cold résumé screens. This req is structurally a cold résumé screen with a domain gate attached, so a cold submit here is the same shape as the three that failed, with an extra filter bolted on. It should not be the plan.

Ranking routes for this specific req, most attainable first:

1. **Artifact-first warm email.** Benchling published Bench-Protocol and wrote publicly about the digital-versus-physical reasoning gap. His LIBERO-PRO work is, in substance, the argument that a widely cited baseline was miscalibrated and that reported gains partly compensate for recipe-induced brittleness. That is the single most relevant thing anyone could say to a team building scientific benchmarks, and it does not require biology to say. A short, concrete note about baseline calibration and gaming resistance on Bench-Protocol, with the recalibration result attached, lands as a peer talking shop rather than a candidate asking for a job. The `#LI-KW1` tag at the bottom of the posting identifies the recruiter's initials and gives a second, lower-signal handle on LinkedIn.
2. **Apply, but say the quiet part in the free text.** If a cold submit does go out, the additional-information field should state the domain gap flat and immediately re-frame: no biology background, deep experience in exactly the failure-mode analysis and judge design the team does, and here is the benchmark-recalibration work. Naming a gap the reader will find anyway costs nothing and buys credibility. Hiding it invites the screen to find it first.
3. **Cold submit with a generic tailored CV as the primary move.** Lowest yield. Not recommended.

**How to sell seniority without lying.**

Lead with what was built, then let the finding arrive as its output: evaluation harnesses and test setups at BMO, scaled to hundreds of synthesized inputs, and that scale is what surfaced systematic risk-downplaying in a tool serving $200B+ AUM. Behaviour invisible at anecdote scale. Then Merlyn as side research: VLM judges producing dense rewards that are hard to game, the RLinf merge, and the LIBERO-PRO recalibration. Three-person collective, nights and weekends, stated flat, no implication of funding or headcount.

Do not lead with the BMO greeter robot. It is a side project he was asked to fix because he knows robots, and it is irrelevant to this team.

**If they downlevel.**

Two plausible moves. First, a redirect to an adjacent Benchling req with a lighter domain bar. Benchling has 48 open reqs including AI and scientific-model engineering roles, and if the recruiter offers that, take it: getting into the building on an infrastructure or platform req beats not getting in at all, and internal transfer to this team later is a real path once the biology is learned on the job. Second, a junior band on the domain gap, which would put the offer near the bottom of the advertised range. That one is a comp problem, not an ego problem, and Block D has the numbers.

**Stretch label (Rule 5).** This is a stretch application. It is worth making, and it must not consume the slot ahead of an evals req where the qualifications are actually met.
