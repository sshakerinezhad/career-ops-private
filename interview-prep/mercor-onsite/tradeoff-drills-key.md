# Trade-off drill keys (plan T7). Open only after your attempt.

The routine, spoken, two minutes per prompt: objective, constraint, two options, failure mode of each, the cheapest experiment that decides it. Attempt first, record it, then open the key and score yourself 0 to 2 on each of six items: objective stated; constraint stated; two real options; a failure mode for each; a deciding experiment that is the cheapest one; a number or regime from a source. 10 to 12 of 12: ready. Under 8: redo next day after a T9 thread on it.

## Keys

1. Synchronous vs fully asynchronous RL for 100-turn agent tasks. **Objective.** Most policy improvement per GPU-hour on 2k to 128k-token trajectories. **Constraint.** A synchronous step waits for the slowest rollout; an async sample comes from an older policy, so its ratio needs the engine's own logprobs, hence TITO. **Options.** A synchronous, on-policy, no correction; vs B fully async: in-flight weight updates, partial rollouts paused and resumed, staleness bounded by (max_staleness_steps + 1) × mini_batch × n_samples. **Fails when.** A fails when lengths are heavy-tailed: GPUs idle on stragglers, one 128k trajectory sets the step; B fails when staleness is uncorrected or silently wrong (re-tokenization drift, engine mismatch): off-policy updates nobody sees. **Deciding experiment.** Overfit a 32-task subset synchronously to prove the loop, then flip async, sweep max_staleness_steps, and plot reward per wall-clock hour against mean |log π_train − log π_infer|. **Number.** Mercor 397B post: concurrency 550 (35B) and 300 (397B); staleness bound 1,024; GPU split 12:4 and 12:8; logprob gap below 0.03.

2. Value model or group baseline for 100-turn tasks. **Objective.** A low-variance per-token advantage from one outcome reward over 100 turns. **Constraint.** A critic is a second policy-sized model (about 16 bytes per parameter with optimizer state) that must predict value deep in a 128k context; the group baseline costs G rollouts instead. **Options.** A value model with GAE, δ_t = r_t + γV(s_{t+1}) − V(s_t), per-token advantages; vs B group baseline, A_i = (r_i − mean)/std over G, broadcast to every token. **Fails when.** A fails when value-at-a-token cannot be learned on long text: biased advantages; B fails when all G rewards are equal (A_i = 0, rollouts wasted), when tiny std amplifies noise, and when one number cannot locate the decisive turn. **Deciding experiment.** On the 32-task subset, count zero-variance groups and check reward climbs with the group baseline; if credit binds, try turn-level advantages before a critic. **Number.** Mercor 397B post: GRPO-family, no value model; batch 32, 8 samples per prompt, non-zero reward variance.

3. token_mean vs prompt_mean loss aggregation on 2k to 128k-token trajectories. **Objective.** Each trajectory moves the gradient by what it teaches, not its length. **Constraint.** One batch mixes 2k and 128k-token trajectories, and an outcome reward gives every token the same advantage. **Options.** A token_mean, (1/Σ|o_i|) Σ_i Σ_t L_{i,t}, per-token weight 1/Σ|o_i|, so a 128k trajectory outweighs a 2k one 64×; vs B prompt_mean, (1/G) Σ_i (1/|o_i|) Σ_t L_{i,t}, per-token weight 1/(G|o_i|), each trajectory counts once. **Fails when.** A fails when lengths are heavy-tailed: a few long rollouts dominate the update; B fails when length carries signal, as in math: DAPO chose token-level so long correct chains count fully, and Dr. GRPO notes 1/|o_i| lets long wrong answers off lightly. **Deciding experiment.** Same seed and data, flip the reduction; compare mean reward with CIs and watch mean trajectory length across training. **Number.** Mercor 397B post: prompt_mean +3.9, 32.54 against a 28.69 baseline of token_mean + GLM-5 loss + rollout_is; DAPO §3 token-level on math; SkyRL DAPO default token_mean.

4. Keep or drop prompts where all 8 rollouts fail. **Objective.** Spend rollouts where the gradient is non-zero without abandoning tasks that become learnable. **Constraint.** A 0-of-8 group has A_i = 0 and teaches nothing; yet it is a noisy estimate of small p, and pass rates drift upward over epochs. **Options.** A drop it (DAPO dynamic sampling: filter all-same groups, resample until the batch is mixed); vs B keep it, now or as a periodic revisit. **Fails when.** A fails when the hardest tasks are never re-probed, so the frontier stays out of reach, and resampling burns budget (SkyRL: max_sample_batches 30); B fails because zero-gradient groups eat budget: your wake term lost across 16 banks. **Deciding experiment.** Re-probe sampled 0-of-8 tasks with one group every few epochs; count entries into the 1 to 2 band; keep it only if held-out reward rises. **Number.** Take-home: E[spread] = (7/8)p(1−p), zero at p = 0 or 1; drift about +0.12 per epoch against 0.095 RMSE; roughly 38% unreachable. DAPO §3.

5. Hard zero vs soft penalty for overlong rollouts. **Objective.** Stop rollouts dying at the context limit without teaching that length is bad. **Constraint.** A truncated rollout has no artifact, so reward defaults to zero; agent-task length is set by the task (2k to 128k), so length penalties hit hard tasks. **Options.** A hard zero, or overlong filtering that drops truncated samples from the loss; vs B DAPO's soft penalty, linear past a soft limit within a buffer. **Fails when.** A fails when truncation is task variance, not misbehaviour: correct long work is punished, everything shortens, filtering discards signal; B fails when the buffer misfits the regime: fine on similar-length math, neutral-to-negative on 2k to 128k agent trajectories. **Deciding experiment.** Measure truncation rate and truncated-rollout reward; then A/B on one seed, plus a nudge-at-20%-context arm, on reward and mean length. **Number.** Mercor 397B post: nudge +3.0, OLF cost 1.5, ALP neutral-to-negative at every setting. SkyRL DAPO: overlong_buffer 512, penalty_factor 1.0.

6. More rollouts per prompt vs more prompts, fixed budget. **Objective.** Most learning signal per rollout with N = prompts × G fixed. **Constraint.** Expected signal per group is ((G−1)/G)p(1−p), barely growing past G = 8, while prompts per step fall as 1/G; held-out generalization comes from coverage. **Options.** A large G, few prompts: better baseline, more mixed groups on hard prompts; vs B small G, many prompts: more distinct gradients, more coverage. **Fails when.** A fails when the prompt set is small: the policy overfits and held-out reward lags; B fails when G is so small the mean is a poor baseline, tiny std blows up advantages, and hard prompts rarely mix. **Deciding experiment.** Fixed N, sweep G over 4, 8, 16 with prompts = N/G, same seeds; track zero-variance groups and held-out reward per rollout. **Number.** Mercor 397B post: 8 samples per prompt, batch 32, 1,928 training tasks, 480 held-out. Primer §8: G typically 8 to 64. Jan 23 post: near-linear trendline in data.

7. LLM judge vs programmatic verifier when both are possible. **Objective.** A correct, hard-to-game reward cheap enough for every rollout. **Constraint.** RL learns whatever the checker rewards; a program is exact and free but brittle to form; a judge reads messy expert criteria but costs a call per criterion and drifts. **Options.** A programmatic verifier (exact match with tolerance, golden file, tests); vs B LLM judge per rubric criterion, binary Met, calibrated on labels. **Fails when.** A fails when correct answers take many forms ($153.5 vs $153.50): false negatives teach format-matching; B fails when the policy learns the judge's taste, when the judge sees the trajectory (self-preference), or when hedging passes. **Deciding experiment.** Both on a labelled held-out slice: precision and recall on Met against expert labels; checker primary where precision nears 100%; watch judge FN and FP during training. **Number.** APEX-Agents 1.1: judge calibrated on 1,407 items, 354 held out; FN 5.3% to 8.0% to catch scattergunning. APEX-Accounting: 97.1% vs three-expert majority, κ 0.857.

8. SFT on a stronger model's trajectories vs RL on your own rollouts. **Objective.** Raise held-out Pass@1 at the lowest cost. **Constraint.** SFT copies the demonstrator and never visits the student's own states, so errors compound (DAgger: O(T²) vs O(T)); RL needs a verifier, rollouts, and prompts the student sometimes passes. **Options.** A SFT on teacher trajectories, cross-entropy on response tokens, capped at the teacher; vs B GRPO on own rollouts with verifier reward, on-policy, uncapped. **Fails when.** A fails off the student's state distribution and by copying teacher errors; B fails when most prompts sit at 0 of 8 (no gradient; cold-start SFT exists for this) or when the verifier is gameable. **Deciding experiment.** Histogram the student's pass rate over training prompts: if most are 0 of 8, SFT into the learnable band, then RL; compare held-out Pass@1 per GPU-hour. **Number.** Mercor 397B post: RL alone, Pass@1 16.11 to 27.29 on 480 held-out tasks. Jan 23 post: GLM 4.6 post-trained "with no SFT warmup, no filtering" nearly doubled.

9. A 2x compute budget: rollouts or training? **Objective.** Most held-out improvement per dollar from a doubled budget. **Constraint.** Agent RL is inference-bound: training is about 6N FLOPs per token against 2N, but a 128k rollout is 128k sequential decodes; concurrency is capped by KV-cache tokens over mean length and by staleness. **Options.** A more rollout GPUs: higher concurrency, more prompts or larger G; vs B more training GPUs: faster steps, larger micro-batches, less staleness. **Fails when.** A fails when concurrency already sits at the staleness ceiling: extra rollouts cannot be consumed and only age; B fails when the trainer already waits on generation: new training GPUs idle. **Deciding experiment.** Profile one step: wall-clock share in generation against training, trainer idle time, which ceiling binds; buy the binding side, then ask whether more tasks beat both. **Number.** Mercor 397B post: GPU split 12:4 (35B) and 12:8 (397B); concurrency 550 and 300; staleness bound 1,024; best knob +3.9 against 10 to 12 for post-training overall.

10. Grading a task whose artifact is a document. **Objective.** Reward the document's state, not the model's account of it. **Constraint.** The artifact is a sandbox file to diff and extract; the judge never sees the trajectory, so it needs the right file handed over; criteria grade true or false. **Options.** A grade the final response text; vs B snapshot-diff the sandbox, extract changed artifacts, auxiliary judge picks the artifact per criterion, per-criterion grading with tolerances. **Fails when.** A fails on narration: "I updated the file" with the file unchanged gets rewarded, so the policy learns to describe, not do; B fails on low-fidelity extraction, a wrong artifact, and per-criterion cost. **Deciding experiment.** Labelled slice with gold outputs: both graders, precision and recall on Met, narration passes counted (claims without a diff), every gold output must pass. **Number.** Mercor 397B post: file-diff over final-response grading; third-party diffing raised fidelity. APEX-Agents: snapshot diff, then an auxiliary judge picks the artifacts per criterion, then the per-criterion judge, which never sees the trajectory; 480 of 480 gold outputs graded correct.

11. DPO vs online RL for a preference dataset. **Objective.** Learn pairwise preferences near the reference, cheaply. **Constraint.** The pairs come from another policy; DPO needs only π_θ and π_ref passes on them: no sampling, critic or reward model; online RL needs a reward model, rollouts, a KL leash. **Options.** A DPO, L = −E log sigmoid(β(log π_θ(y_w)/π_ref(y_w) − log π_θ(y_l)/π_ref(y_l))); vs B reward model on the pairs, then PPO or GRPO on r_RM − β KL. **Fails when.** A fails when the pairs sit off-policy (the model never sees its own mistakes) and by likelihood displacement, both responses dropping while ordering holds; B fails by overoptimization: proxy reward rises, gold reward peaks and falls. **Deciding experiment.** DPO first, tracking held-out preference accuracy, judged win rate against π_ref, and KL; if fresh samples fall outside the pairs, go iterative DPO or online RL. **Number.** Rafailov 2023: β sets distance to π_ref; PPO ε ≈ 0.2; Gao et al. 2022 overoptimization. Mercor used no DPO in the 397B post.

12. Process reward model vs outcome reward for a 40-step task. **Objective.** Credit the decisive step among 40 without inviting credit farming. **Constraint.** One outcome number gives every step the same advantage, variance growing with horizon; a PRM needs step labels (human or Monte Carlo from branches) and is exploitable. **Options.** A outcome reward with the group baseline, unbiased, noisy per step; vs B a PRM per step, dense, lower variance, biased and gameable. **Fails when.** A fails at low pass rates over long horizons: tiny signal per rollout, steps credited for nothing; B fails when step scores are not γΦ(s′) − Φ(s): the policy farms step credit while failing tasks (Catan VP trap). **Deciding experiment.** Keep outcome reward; try turn-level advantages first (step-wise makes each turn a sample); only then branch-label a few steps, train a PRM, compare held-out pass rate. **Number.** none in the sources for PRM against outcome; say so aloud. Regime: Mercor's trial reward is per-criterion outcome (recipe repo); Ng, Harada, Russell 1999 for shaping.

13. LoRA vs full fine-tune under a memory cap. **Objective.** Fit in memory and still get the update the task needs. **Constraint.** Full fine-tuning holds weights, gradients and Adam states, about 16 bytes per parameter; LoRA freezes W and trains B (d×r), A (r×k): r(d+k) parameters, rank r. **Options.** A LoRA, h = Wx + (α/r)BAx, B starts at zero, merges to W + BA; vs B full fine-tune fitted by sharding, offload, activation checkpointing. **Fails when.** A fails when the change must spread across the network: LIBERO-PRO, LoRA 15 to 21 against 42 at matched hyperparameters, read as undoing memorization; B fails when it does not fit and the fixes cost step time. **Deciding experiment.** Same data, matched hyperparameters, sweep r against one checkpointed full fine-tune on a slice; if high-r LoRA still lags, the gap is expressivity, not tuning. **Number.** Your own: LoRA 15 to 21 against FFT 42, batch 64, LR 1e-5, a finding, not a verdict; Edward Hu invented LoRA and leads Mercor training.

14. Offline logged trajectories vs on-policy rollouts. **Objective.** Improve the policy from data already held rather than fresh rollouts. **Constraint.** Logs come from another policy, so the policy gradient's on-policy state distribution fails; long-sequence ratios π_θ/π_behaviour are unbounded, and no ratio reaches unvisited states. **Options.** A offline: filter the logs and SFT the keepers, conservative offline RL (CQL, IQL), or clipped-ratio policy gradient; vs B on-policy GRPO with bounded staleness only. **Fails when.** A fails when the logs sit far from the current policy: ratios blow up or clip to nothing, and the deadly triad (bootstrapping, off-policy, approximation) bites; B fails on cost, and goes off-policy anyway without TITO and the logprob check. **Deciding experiment.** One pass scoring logged tokens under the current policy; if mean |log π_θ − log π_behaviour| far exceeds the tolerated band, filtered SFT, then on-policy. **Number.** Mercor 397B post: healthy logprob gap below 0.03; staleness bound 1,024. SkyRL: TIS token clip 2.0, sequence clip 5.0. DAgger: O(T²) to O(T).

15. Training compute vs test-time compute at a fixed budget. **Objective.** Highest pass rate per dollar over the expected query volume. **Constraint.** Training is paid once and amortised over every call; test-time compute is paid per query and needs a selector, since best-of-n without a verifier is Pass@k, a ceiling. **Options.** A RL on the task distribution, raising Pass@1 for every call; vs B inference: n samples with verifier selection, bigger budgets. **Fails when.** A fails at low query volume, or when RL only sharpens what the base had at k, the Yue et al. 2025 pass@k result (verify); B fails without a reliable selector (Pass@8 is "an indicative ceiling"; repeats aggregate into Pass@1). **Deciding experiment.** Pass rate against cost per task: base at n of 1, 4, 8 with verifier selection versus trained at n = 1; the crossover decides. **Number.** APEX-Agents Jan post: under 25% at one try, 40% with 8. Mercor 397B post: Pass@1 16.11 to 27.29. APEX-Accounting: Fable 5 $36.00 per run, Qwen3.5-397B $0.68.
