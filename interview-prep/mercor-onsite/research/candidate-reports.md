## Reports

R1. Blind, "Mercor swe interview experience", Aug 26, 2026, SWE onsite, technical role, TC 200K. "They made you independently work and then review with someone. First question was about a simple concept but disguised as a real world use case (common) but they actually wanted it to work like a real product. Instead of you implementing the actual logic based on theory and the problem and use dummy inputs to test it, they wanted it to work end to end. So in an hour you had to figure out data processing of the input and then actually implement the other stuff, no stubbing. Second part wanted you to use AI to improve some baseline system and you can test against theirs to see if your result were better. The expectation was you use ai to quickly vibe code a solution but actually guide the architecture and decision to beat theirs, which my solution did. So after over an hour of vibe coding, my interviewer had no questions about my approach and thoughts around architecture and design, but just wanted to review the code which i honestly had no idea about bc I was SUPPOSED TO BE VIBE CODING." ## What each session most plausibly contains

Session 1 (eval practical): the "work independently, then review with someone" pattern is consistent across 2025–26 reports (R1, R2, R3, R5, R6); R1: end-to-end working artifact, "no stubbing", and a line-by-line review of AI-written code. Closest content analog: R10's eval-harness prompt. INFERRED from apex@mercor.com and the JD: APEX-style prompts with rubrics or references; per-criterion LLM-judge grading; multiple runs with bootstrap CIs; Pass@1 vs Pass@k; judge-vs-reference agreement; failure categorization. The Research Scientist, APEX Benchmarks JD spells out the reporting bar: "confidence intervals, inter-rater agreement, human vs. model-as-judge calibration, held-out splits, and the failure analysis that explains why a model scored the way it did"; "you don't ship a number you can't defend." No first-hand report of this exact session exists.

Session 2 (algorithms): existence confirmed in every 2026 loop description; no one has described its content. Adjacent Mercor prompts: ranking under a slow/noisy comparator, DAG centrality with expected value, sorting complexity and lower bounds, probability with ties, grid shortest path. INFERRED: verbal complexity/design discussion on a Mercor-shaped problem with follow-ups on cost, wall-clock and correctness.

Session 3 (post-training): R6 is the only content report (GRPO round, GRPO-scheduler take-home, "GRPO vs PPO"). R10's noisy-data and compact-GPT prompts are adjacent. INFERRED: GRPO/PPO/DAPO/DPPO mechanisms, rubric vs verifiable rewards and reward hacking, off-policy drift from async and tokenization, data quality and contamination, infra trade-offs.

## Litmus format (public)

litmushiring.com/neo: "A Litmus assessment is a real, open-ended engineering project that you build in your own environment using your own tools"; "AI is allowed and encouraged"; `litmus init` loads it locally; "a project brief with requirements but no single right answer"; "45 to 75 minutes"; `litmus submit`. litmus.build: "tracks everything from file iteration, to commit history, to AI prompts"; "Every submission runs live sandbox against a deterministic harness for correctness. Configurable LLM graders evaluate qualitative dimensions like process and architecture"; "every candidate records a short walkthrough explaining their decisions" (10-minute total timer, 3-minute per question). YC S26; clients Mercor and Composio; grades "outcome, process, and tool fluency, down to AI prompting". Matches R6's "record answers to 5 questions in 10 mins".
